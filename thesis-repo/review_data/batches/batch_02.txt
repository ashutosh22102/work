Title: The impact of design thinking and artificial intelligence capabilities on performance: The role of new product development decision-making agility
Journal: Journal of Business Research (2025)
Abstract: Design thinking and artificial intelligence (AI) capabilities are gaining prominence in today’s dynamic markets. However, research gaps remain regarding their influence on the outcomes of new product development (NPD), such as decision-making agility, and the structural conditions facilitating or impeding their effective implementation. Considering design thinking as a dynamic capability and AI capabilities as technology-driven innovation enablers, this study examines their impact on NPD performance via NPD decision-making agility. An empirical investigation using data collected from 230 U.S. firms shows that design thinking and AI capabilities positively influence agility, which in turn drives NPD performance. This study also uncovers that the moderating role of organizational formalization attenuates the impact of design thinking on NPD decision-making agility but strengthens the impact of AI capabilities on NPD decision-making agility. These findings provide NPD managers with insights into using these capabilities to enhance agility and improve NPD performance in the organizational context.

Title: Artificial intelligence in enhancing entrepreneurial performance: The mediating role of innovation capability and sustainable entrepreneurship
Journal: Computers in Human Behavior Reports (2026)
Abstract: Artificial Intelligence (AI) technology is now a transformative force, creating unparalleled opportunities for innovation, efficiency, and competitive advantage in sustainable entrepreneurship, thereby enhancing entrepreneurial performance. The objective of the study is to explore the direct effect of artificial intelligence usage (AIU) on entrepreneurial performance (EP) and the indirect effects through the mediating variables of innovation capability (IC) and sustainable entrepreneurship (SE). Based on the ‘positivism’ research philosophy, this study has used a quantitative methodology and utilized multi-level convenience sampling to collect data from 295 respondents. This research developed a conceptual framework and tested the hypothesized relationships by applying partial least squares structural equation modeling (PLS-SEM). The study’s results show that four hypotheses focusing on direct relationships are supported. The hypothesized relation between IC and EP is not supported by the empirical data, and IC does not mediate the relation between AIU and SE. From a theoretical perspective, the study extends the Resource-Based View theory and the Dynamic Capabilities Theory. In practice, this study’s results will guide policymakers, entrepreneurs, SME owners, and practitioners in designing strategies, providing the necessary information, and making capability investments to understand the consequences of developing AI capabilities within organizational practices, thereby magnifying innovation, sustainability, and competitiveness. As future research directions, this study has suggested a longitudinal study across different cultural, institutional, and economic contexts and geographical regions to examine the use and influence of AI on entrepreneurial performance.

Title: Artificial Intelligence Capabilities and Competitive Advantage:  The Mediating Role of Organizational Agility and the Moderating  Effect of Industry Dynamism
Journal: Journal of Logistics, Informatics and Service Science (2026)
Abstract: Artificial intelligence capabilities are becoming more and more important drivers for improving information processing, operational coordination, and service responsiveness as digital transformation progresses.Although artificial intelligence is commonly perceived as a way to enhance organizational performance, there is currently conflicting evidence regarding how artificial intelligence capabilities actually result in competitive advantage, especially when it comes to the impact of organizational agility and varying degrees of industry dynamism in businesses operating in emerging markets.Analyzing survey data from 191 Jordanian manufacturing companies in the first quarter of 2025, structural equation modeling results show that organizational agility (β = 0.44) and competitive advantage (β = 0.38) are positively impacted by artificial intelligence capabilities.Additionally, the analysis shows that the relationship between competitive advantage and artificial intelligence skills is partially mediated by organizational agility (β = 0.21).Additionally, the direct association between competitive advantage and AI skills is positively moderated by industry dynamism (β = 0.19).By demonstrating that artificial intelligence capabilities result in larger strategic benefits when they are matched with organizational response procedures and implemented in highly dynamic industry situations, this study expands on studies on informatics and service science.

=== R031 ===
Title: Investigating the microfoundation of dynamic managerial and organizational capabilities for business model innovation: a mediated-moderated model of artificial intelligence
Journal: Journal of Innovation & Knowledge (2026)
Abstract: In rapidly evolving digital environments, organizations must develop dynamic capabilities to drive business model innovation (BMI). This study examines how dynamic managerial capabilities (DMCs), including internal advice-seeking, external advice-seeking, and collaborative sensemaking, translate into BMI through two AI-driven organizational capabilities: artificial intelligence integration capability (AIIC) and AI-augmented coordination flexibility capability. Guided by the dynamic capabilities view (DCV), we construct and empirically test a multilevel model using survey data from 517 managers purposively sampled from Chinese firms undergoing digital transformation. Results from partial least squares structural equation modeling indicate that internal advice-seeking has the strongest direct effect on BMI. Conversely, external advice-seeking most strongly influences AIIC. AI integration enhances coordination flexibility, which in turn drives BMI. The findings support a serial mediation model in which DMCs influence BMI through AI integration and coordination flexibility. Moreover, a data-driven decision-making orientation significantly strengthens the relationship between coordination flexibility and BMI, underscoring the importance of organizational mindset. This study advances the DCV by introducing and validating two AI-driven capabilities that link sensing, seizing, and transforming in strategic renewal. Managers who actively seek external insights, foster collaborative sensemaking, and lead data-driven teams are best positioned to leverage AI for sustainable BMI.

=== R037 ===
Title: AI-enabled business model transformation and entrepreneurial quality: The moderating effect of technological turbulence
Journal: Telematics and Informatics Reports (2026)
Abstract: Highlights: AI adoption and capabilities drive business model transformation in startups. Business model transformation enhances entrepreneurial quality dimensions. Technological turbulence weakens links between transformation and innovation outcomes. AI impacts are fully mediated through business model transformation pathways. Abstract: This study examines how AI adoption and capabilities shape entrepreneurial quality through business model transformation under technological turbulence. Drawing on the dynamic capability view, we test a three-wave panel model using data from 218 Vietnamese startups in three national innovation parks. SmartPLS results show that AI adoption and AI capabilities significantly enhance business model transformation, which in turn improves risk-taking, pro-activeness, innovativeness, and entrepreneurial activity frequency. Technological turbulence weakens the effects of business model transformation on risk-taking, pro-activeness, and innovativeness, but not on entrepreneurial activity frequency. Mediation analysis confirms business model transformation as a significant partial mediator.

=== R040 ===
Title: Leveraging AI-enabled knowledge capabilities for new product performance: Evidence from manufacturing SMEs in emerging economies
Journal: Industrial Marketing Management (2026)
Abstract: Emerging market manufacturing small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) struggle to translate artificial intelligence (AI) investments into innovation outcomes. This study investigates how AI-enabled knowledge capabilities drive new product performance by employing a sequential mixed-methods design with 261 manufacturing SMEs. Our findings reveal that AI-enabled knowledge capabilities operate through dual pathways: directly enhancing performance and indirectly through digital product innovation radicalness. Furthermore, this study identifies digital sustainability leadership as a critical boundary condition acting as both mediator and moderator, fundamentally reconfiguring how AI generates value. This study makes significant contributions to dynamic capabilities theory and the knowledge-based view. By positioning AI-enabled knowledge capabilities as critical dynamic capabilities, this research clarifies the transformation pathways through which AI generates competitive advantages and offer actionable insights for managers navigating resource-constrained environments.
